## The stack (ask in this order)

### 1. Knowledge substrate for FedAI / the Authority — graph-of-graphs verification

**Slot:** Ontology + provenance layer that FedAI agents actually run on. Cross-ministry “collect once, share securely.” Context that does not silently corrupt. Human-inspectable graph of graphs.

**Why you:** This is the thread’s #1 and your 10-year public thesis. UAE is standing up FedAI and a National AI Gateway *now* (workshop Aug 2026). Agents coordinating across entities without a shared ontology will hallucinate jurisdiction. ADGM already proved a regulatory KG is possible; the federal layer does not have one.

**Prototype to offer (7–10 days, public data only):**

> One priority **service ontology** (pick: golden-visa / corporate-tax registration / food-import clearance — all have public FTA/ICP/MoCCAE rules) **plus** a **graph-of-graphs inspector**: every agent claim is a node with `source`, `timestamp`, `ministry`, `confidence`, and a human override edge. Residuality card on the side: if the agent is wrong, what is the *smallest* control (from `uae.md` C-17 rights registry / C-13 digital degrade-mode).

**OSINT/gov inputs:** Bayanat, FTA guidance, ICP visa rules, MoJ open data, ADGM public KG as a *pattern* not a scrape of their internals.

**Planet:** not required for v1. Attach later as a `grounded_by` edge on any claim about a physical site (project 3).

**Ask the friend:** *Who owns the ontology/data-model workstream under the Authority or the Agentic AI technology-and-data track? I can prototype the verification layer for one service, not a strategy deck.*

### 2. Task-classification + liability graph (“human leads, AI enables”)

**Slot:** The live two-year clock to classify which government tasks an agent may **execute** vs only **recommend**. Liability (provider vs operator), memory reliability, objection/correction path (already in the Aug 2026 service-design guide).

**Why you:** Your liability-vs-safeguards posts map onto the exact question no government has answered in public. Residuality gives the method: attractor (do-nothing if the agent acts wrongly) → smallest residue (human gate, not a new ministry).

**Prototype:**

> A **task graph** for one ministry-shaped public process (e.g. FTA CIT registration, or ICP visa grant): nodes = tasks; edges = `may_execute` / `may_recommend` / `must_escalate`; each edge carries **liability owner**, **evidence required**, **sunset** (C-17). 3D/AR view optional but on-brand — even a 2D inspector that a non-specialist can correct is the point.

**Gov inputs:** Unified agentic-service guide (21 Aug 2026); Cabinet first-package of services (May 2026); FAHR/80k competency categories (Leadership / Technical / Specialist / General / Train-the-trainer).

**Ask:** *Is task classification a Cabinet Affairs workstream or Authority standards? I can show a residuality-shaped classification for one service in a week.*

### 4. Regulatory Intelligence as a governed legal graph

**Slot:** Regulatory Intelligence Office (Cabinet GS, Apr 2025) wants AI to draft/update law, cut cycle time ~70%, human still the arbiter. ADGM/FSRA already shipped a **regulatory knowledge graph** (ClauseMatch, Neo4j public dump, taxonomy ENT–PERM–ACT–RISK–MIT). Qanooni-style legal data graphs exist commercially for UAE sources.

**Why you:** “Ontologies as storming of meaning”; graphs so humans can verify AI outputs; Deutsch/critical-rationalist allergy to ungrounded generation. Black-box law-drafting is the failure mode.

**Prototype:**

> **One statute family** (federal CIT decree + FTA circulars, or nuclear/FANR public licences) as a citation graph: `amends`, `delegates`, `conflicts_with`, `implements`. Agent may *propose* an amendment node; it cannot *commit* without a human edge. Residuality watch: emergency measures without sunsets (S-62 / C-17).

**Ask:** *Does the Authority or Cabinet GS need a graph-native legislative substrate, not another LLM wrapper?*
